longDescription: |
  Anadolu Hisarı is the smaller, more weathered sister of Rumeli Hisarı across the Bosphorus — and the older of the two by 40 years. It was built in 1393 by Sultan Bayezid I as the Ottoman foothold on the Asian shore, a small but strategic fortress to control Bosphorus traffic and prepare the ground for what would become the 1453 conquest. The square keep and the crenellated walls are still there, built right at the water's edge on a rocky outcrop.

  Compared to the showpiece Rumeli fortress, Anadolu Hisarı is raw, low, and atmospheric — more like an archaeological site than a museum. The interior tower is closed to visitors, but you can walk the outer walls for free and explore the small park around it. The setting on the Asian Bosphorus, with the Rumeli fortress clearly visible directly across the narrowest part of the strait, makes the geography of the conquest immediate and easy to grasp.

  Combine with a visit to Küçüksu Palace (3 km north along the Bosphorus) and a stop at one of the waterfront fish restaurants in the Anadolu Hisarı neighborhood itself. A short walk down to the ferry terminal also gives easy access back to the European side.
