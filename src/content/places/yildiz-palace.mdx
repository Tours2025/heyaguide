longDescription: |
  Yıldız Palace was the last of the Ottoman imperial residences, used by Abdülhamid II (r. 1876-1909) when Dolmabahçe felt too grand and exposed. The compound sits on a hill between Beşiktaş and Ortaköy, on the European shore of the Bosphorus. It's much less visited than its sibling Dolmabahçe, partly because it's harder to find, partly because the experience is more about the gardens and grounds than the rooms.

  The complex contains several pavilions — Yıldız Şale (the main residence, with two-story reception halls and the famous writing desk where the Sultan received the first telegraph from Berlin), Yıldız Sale Kiosk, Malta Kiosk (where the Sultan met Kaiser Wilhelm II in 1898), the Writing Pavilion, and a small theater built for private opera performances. The 30-hectare gardens with magnolia, oak, and conifer groves are the actual highlight.

  The Yıldız Çay Bahçesi (tea garden) inside the grounds is one of the most beautiful outdoor tea gardens in the city. The Imperial Porcelain Factory, also inside the compound, was the Sultan's atelier for hand-painted porcelain — there's a small but exquisite collection of original Yıldız porcelain on display.

  Closed Mondays. ~10 EUR. Allow 90 minutes — most of it outdoors. Combine with a walk along the Bosphorus shore to Ortaköy.
