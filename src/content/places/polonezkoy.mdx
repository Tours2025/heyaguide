longDescription: |
  Polonezköy — "Polish Village" — was founded in 1842 by Polish refugees who fled the November Uprising against Russian rule and were given land by Sultan Abdülmecid I. For 180 years the descendants of those settlers kept the village alive; today the population is mostly Turkish (the Polish families intermarried or left), but the name and a small museum preserve the heritage.

  The reason to come is the forest. Polonezköy sits in a 3,000-decare nature park on the Asian side, an hour's drive north of the city. The air is cooler, the trees are old (oak, chestnut, pine), and the silence is real. The main street is lined with country restaurants serving Turkish breakfast, gözleme, fresh trout, and forest mushrooms — most have outdoor garden tables under the trees. Walk it off on one of the marked trails in the nature park, or visit the small museum and the Polish cemetery.

  It's a half-day or full-day trip rather than a quick stop. Best with a rental car or a tour, but there are minibuses from Çekmeköy on weekends. Less crowded on weekdays.
