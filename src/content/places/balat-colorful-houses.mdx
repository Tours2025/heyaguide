longDescription: |
  Balat was historically a Jewish quarter — Sephardic families expelled from Spain settled here in the 1490s, and for centuries it was one of the most cosmopolitan corners of the city, with Greek, Armenian, and Jewish communities living side by side. The neighborhood still shows traces of that history in the synagogues tucked between houses, the Fener Greek Patriarchate on the next hill, and the Bulgarian Iron Church on the bay below.

  Today the streets are quieter than the old city, and the visual reward is the row of pastel wooden houses on the slope of Merdivenli Yokuşu (the "stair street") and along Vodina Caddesi. The colors — pale yellow, dusty pink, mint green, faded terracotta — are not the original paint; they've been refreshed over the last decade as Balat has become a favorite of photographers and weekend strollers. A few tea gardens and antique shops have opened in the old stone ground floors.

  Best at golden hour, when the slanted sun makes the colors sing. Pair with the Iron Church (15 min walk downhill) and the Patriarchate (10 min uphill). On Sundays the Balat flea market (see separate listing) sets up along the main street.
