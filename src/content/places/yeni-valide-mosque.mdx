longDescription: |
  Yeni Valide Mosque was commissioned in 1597 by Sultana Safiye, but the project dragged on for decades — the final dome wasn't completed until 1665, under another valide sultan (Valide Turhan Hatice, mother of Mehmed IV). The result is a large, late-classical Ottoman mosque with a tall central dome, two minarets, and a generous courtyard shaded by old plane trees.

  The interior is more reserved than the Blue Mosque or Süleymaniye — pale marble, painted calligraphy, and a few high windows that let in cool Bosphorus-side light. What makes it special is the setting: it's on a hill in the heart of Üsküdar, and from the courtyard wall you can see the Bosphorus, the Maiden's Tower, and the Asian shore stretching out toward the Bosphorus bridges.

  The neighborhood around it is one of Istanbul's most authentically local: tea gardens, fish sandwiches (balık ekmek) at the waterfront below, and the pier where the historic Üsküdar–Eminönü ferries still leave every 20 minutes. The Şemsi Pasha Mosque is 5 minutes' walk downhill.
