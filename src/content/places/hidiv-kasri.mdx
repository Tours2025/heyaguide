longDescription: |
  Hidiv Kasrı was built in 1907 for Abbas Hilmi II, the last khedive of Egypt, as his Istanbul retreat. The architect was Delfo Seminati, an Italian working in the new Art Nouveau mode — and the result is one of the most unusual buildings on the Bosphorus: a small, almost fairy-tale mansion with carved stone, wrought-iron balconies, and stained-glass windows set in a hillside garden.

  The pavilion is now a small museum run by the Istanbul municipality. Inside, you can tour the ground-floor reception rooms with their original Art Nouveau furniture, the grand staircase with painted panels, and the upper-floor private quarters. The collection is light — period furniture, photos of the khedive, and a small display on the Bosphorus villas. The real reason to come is the setting: a 170-decare wooded park, well above the Bosphorus, with panoramic views across the strait and down to the Bosphorus Bridge.

  The park is the best part and is free. Locals come here for sunset picnics under the old cypresses. The tea garden next to the pavilion serves a glass of tea and a simit for a few lira. A 30-minute visit is enough for the building; allow an hour or two if you also want to walk the park paths.
