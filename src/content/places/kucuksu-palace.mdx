longDescription: |
  Küçüksu Kasrı is the smallest of the three imperial Bosphorus palaces (the others being Dolmabahçe and Beylerbeyi), and the most ornate. It was built in 1856 by Sultan Abdülmecid I in a florid rococo style — a small waterfront villa with marble facades, baroque scrollwork, and a gilt interior dripping with chandeliers and trompe-l'œil ceilings. The sultans used it as a rest stop during day-long Bosphorus cruises: they'd row up from Dolmabahçe, stop for a few hours, then continue to the upper Bosphorus.

  The interior is arranged around a central reception hall with a glass-domed ceiling, off which open the sultan's bedroom (with a small private bath cut from a single block of marble), the valide sultan's quarters, the writing room, and a small music room. The decorative program is over the top in the best sense — pink marble, mother-of-pearl inlay, and acres of gold leaf.

  Out back, the private water gate opens directly onto the Bosphorus; you can still see the wrought-iron mooring posts where the imperial caiques tied up. Plan 45–60 minutes, and combine with a stop at Anadolu Hisarı (2 km south) for a Bosphorus-history day.
