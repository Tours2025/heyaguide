longDescription: |
  The Şerefiye Cistern (Şerefiye Sarnıcı) is the smaller, older sister of the Basilica Cistern — and was, until its 2018 reopening after a 7-year restoration, almost completely unknown to visitors. It was built by the Roman emperor Theodosius I in the 4th century, two centuries before Justinian's Basilica Cistern, as part of the city's water infrastructure.

  The cistern is small (15m x 30m, about a third of the Basilica) but tall — the columns rise nearly 9 meters above the water, with a vaulted ceiling you can see at certain angles. The columns are mostly plain, with Corinthian and other capitals mixed — same recycling-of-Roman-ruins logic as the Basilica Cistern.

  What makes Şerefiye special is the 2018 restoration, which added a subtle and elegant immersive light installation — the cistern's stone walls and columns are gently illuminated in shifting colors and patterns, and an evocative audio track plays. It's a different mood from the Basilica Cistern: less archaeological, more contemplative.

  The cistern sits in a small park, near the Piyerloti Hill exit of the tram line and the ancient column of Marcian — easy to combine with a walking tour of the old city. ~12 EUR, daily 09:00-19:00.
